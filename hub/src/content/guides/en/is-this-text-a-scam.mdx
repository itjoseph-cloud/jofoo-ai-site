<TLDR>Ask whether you expected the text, what it asks for, and whether the link fits the company. Do not reply or share details. Confirm on a number or website you already know, then report the text in your messaging app.</TLDR>

<Problem>A text arrives from a sender you do not know. It says a parcel is waiting, your account has a problem, or you won a prize. It asks you to tap a link. You are not sure what to do.</Problem>

<Why>
Scam texts try to trick you into giving away personal details. The FTC lists passwords, account numbers and Social Security numbers.

Some links lead to look-alike websites. Others may install harmful software on your phone.

Your phone may filter some messages. None of the pages we read says a filter catches every scam, so the checks below still matter.
</Why>

<Fix>Run five checks on the text. If it looks like a scam, report it, then turn on your phone's filters. The five checks are our own grouping of advice from the FTC and Saudi Arabia's Communications, Space and Technology Commission (CST).</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Check 1: Were you expecting it?** Ask if you know the sender or were waiting for this message. An unknown sender is a reason to pause. So is a sender name that looks like an altered version of a delivery company's name. The CST lists both as warning signs.</Step>
<Step>**Check 2: What does it ask for?** The FTC lists common lures. These are free prizes and gift cards, "suspicious activity" on your account, and fake package delivery notices. The CST adds fake job offers, requests to update your data, a claim that your ATM card is stopped, and prize claims. Any text that asks for personal or card details deserves a pause.</Step>
<Step>**Check 3: Does the link fit the company?** Do not tap the link to find out. The FTC says some links lead to look-alike sites. The CST says a random link that has nothing to do with the shipping company named in the text is a warning sign.</Step>
<Step>**Check 4: Do not reply or share data.** The CST advises you not to reply to a scam message and not to share personal details with people you do not trust. On iPhone, replying also stops you from reporting the message later.</Step>
<Step>**Check 5: Confirm on your own.** The FTC says to contact the company using a phone number or website you know is real. Do not use the details in the text. The FTC also says legitimate companies will not ask for account information by text.</Step>
<Step>**Report it on iPhone.** If you have not opened the message, swipe left on it, tap Delete, then tap Delete and Report Spam. If you opened it, tap the Report Spam link at the bottom, then tap Delete and Report Spam. {/* CHECK: Apple pages show iOS 26 and iOS 27 variants; confirm the labels on a real iPhone and note the iOS version. */} Apple says iMessage reports go to Apple. For SMS, MMS or RCS, a report may be shared with your carrier, depending on your carrier and country.</Step>
<Step>**Block the sender on iPhone.** Apple says reporting does not stop the sender from texting you. To block, open the conversation and tap the person's icon at the top. Tap Info, scroll down, then tap Block Contact.</Step>
<Step>**Report it in Google Messages.** If Google Messages shows a suspected spam or scam warning, tap Report spam, or Report not spam if the warning is wrong. {/* CHECK: the Google help page we read showed only the computer steps. The Android phone steps for report and block are not recorded; confirm on a real phone and add the exact labels. */} Google says a spam report also blocks the sender and moves the message to your Spam & blocked folder. On a computer, Google's page gives this path: next to the person, click More options, then Block & report spam, then OK.</Step>
<Step>**Report it to your carrier or authority.** Pick the line for your country. In the US, copy the text and forward it to 7726 (SPAM). The FTC says this helps your wireless provider spot and block similar messages. You can also report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. In Saudi Arabia, forward the text to 330330. The CST page says this service is free for subscribers of all providers. {/* CHECK: other countries are not researched (UAE, Egypt and others). Keep this region-labelled and add sources before any other country is named. */} If you are elsewhere, check your carrier's official page.</Step>
<Step>**Turn on filtering on iPhone.** Apple says spam filtering is on by default. To check it, open Messages, tap the Filters button, tap Manage Filtering, and look at Filter spam. {/* CHECK: Screen Unknown Senders is off by default in iOS 26 per Apple; confirm the default and menu labels on the iOS version used in the recording. */} In the same place you can turn on Screen Unknown Senders. When it is on, Apple says you can mark a sender as known or add them to Contacts, and only then can you open links they have sent. Apple also says filtering does not apply to anyone you have replied to three times or more.</Step>
<Step>**Check spam protection in Google Messages.** Open Google Messages. Tap your profile picture or initials at the top right. Tap Messages settings, then Protection & Safety, then Spam and scam protection. Google lists Enhanced, Standard and No protection, and says Standard is on by default. {/* CHECK: Google says the setting appears only if it is available on your device; confirm on a Samsung phone and with the Arabic interface. */} If you do not see it, it may not be available on your phone.</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Prompt label="Message to a family member">Hi [name]. I got a text from [sender name or number] that says [describe it in your own words, for example: a parcel is waiting]. Did you send it, or were you expecting it? I have not tapped the link or replied. I will check with the company on a number I already know.</Prompt>

<Tip>Use this message when a text seems to come from someone you know. Describe the text in your own words instead of pasting the link. Apple and Google show different steps on different software versions, so follow the help page for your version if a button is not where we say.</Tip>

<Warning>Do not reply to a text you think is a scam. On iPhone, you cannot report a message after you reply to it. Replying can also stop Apple's filtering for that sender after three replies.</Warning>

<Safety>
Never send your password, a code, a card number or an account number by text. Do not paste them anywhere to "check" a message. {/* CHECK: editorial advice, not from the FTC, Apple or Google. */} Our advice is also to keep personal details, full phone numbers, codes and links you did not open out of any chatbot.

Reporting in Google Messages sends the last 10 incoming messages from the sender to Google. Google says its employees and contractors may review the report and that reports help train AI models. Your past replies are not sent. Google also says reporting is optional, and you can block a number without reporting it.

This guide covers recognising and reporting a suspicious text. {/* CHECK: no source read covers what to do after you have tapped, replied or shared details; add a primary source or keep this limit. */} It does not cover what to do if you have already shared details.
</Safety>
