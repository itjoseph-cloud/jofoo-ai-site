<TLDR>Keep six kinds of details out of your chats: passwords and codes, card and bank numbers, ID numbers, health records, other people's private details, and confidential work files. That list is ours, not OpenAI's. Then turn off Improve the model for everyone in Data controls. OpenAI says it then won't use your new chats to train its models, unless you give thumbs-up or thumbs-down feedback on a reply.</TLDR>

<Problem>You are about to paste something into ChatGPT. It might be a work note, a form, or a message with someone's details in it. You wonder if it is okay to type, and what the settings really change.</Problem>

<Why>
On a personal account, including the free plan, OpenAI says it may use your content to train its models. Data sharing for this is turned on by default, and you can turn it off while keeping your chat history.

OpenAI also says a limited number of authorised staff may access your content, only for stated reasons: investigating abuse or a security incident, giving support, handling legal matters, and improving models unless you have opted out. OpenAI says it does not sell your data or share your ChatGPT conversations with advertisers.

OpenAI's plain-language advice is short: "Please do not enter sensitive information that you would not want reviewed or used." The FTC makes a wider point about personal information online: it says your personal information is valuable.
</Why>

<Fix>Keep sensitive details out of the chat first. Then change the settings you control. Settings reduce what happens to your new chats. They do not make a chat private.</Fix>

## Our List: What to Keep Out of Chats

This list is our own editorial advice. OpenAI does not publish it. The only OpenAI sentence we quote is the one above. {/* CHECK: founder approves list */}

- Passwords and one-time codes.
- Full card or bank numbers.
- Government ID numbers.
- Health records.
- Other people's private details.
- Confidential work or client files.

## Change the Settings

These steps follow OpenAI's help pages, which we read on 2026-11-05. Menu names can change, so follow what you see on screen. {/* CHECK: all menu paths came from OpenAI help pages (some cached); re-confirm in the live demo. */} Each step is on its own, so you can stop after any of them.

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Turn off model training on the web.** Open ChatGPT and sign in. Open your account menu and select Settings. Select Data controls. Turn off Improve the model for everyone, then select Done. OpenAI says this does not delete or hide your saved chats.</Step>
<Step>**Turn it off on your phone too.** In the iOS or Android app, open the sidebar and tap your profile icon. Tap Data controls and turn off Improve the model for everyone. {/* CHECK: confirm the phone path on the founder's device. */} Voice mode has its own control for sharing data for model training. {/* CHECK: voice-mode control location is unknown until the demo. */} We have not yet confirmed where it sits in the app, so look for it if you use voice mode.</Step>
<Step>**Use Temporary chat for one-off questions.** Open a new chat and select Temporary. Choose Personalized or Unpersonalized before you send your first message. You cannot change this after the chat starts. OpenAI says an unsaved temporary chat cannot be reopened from your history after you leave it. If you save it, it becomes a regular chat and your model-improvement setting applies.</Step>
<Step>**Check Memory.** Open Settings, select Personalization, then select Memory. OpenAI says memory controls vary by plan, region, platform and workspace, so a setting may not show for your account yet. {/* CHECK: whether Memory and Personalization show on a free account is unknown until the demo. */} OpenAI also says sensitive information can appear in Memory if you share it with ChatGPT. Read what is there. You can also ask ChatGPT what it remembers about you.</Step>
<Step>**Delete one chat you no longer need.** On the web, open your chat history, select the more options menu (•••) for that chat, select Delete, and confirm. {/* CHECK: confirm the exact web delete path in the live demo. */} In the app, select the menu icon (two horizontal lines), press and hold the chat title, then select Delete. The chat leaves your account view at once. You cannot restore it. OpenAI says deleting a chat alone does not necessarily delete a separate saved memory made from it, so check Memory again.</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Tip>OpenAI says there are two equal ways to opt out of training: turn off Improve the model for everyone in Settings, then Data controls, or select Do not train on my content in its Privacy Portal. Either one is enough. This guide covers personal accounts. Business, Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare accounts work differently.</Tip>

<Tip>Archiving is not deleting. OpenAI says an archived chat leaves the regular sidebar but stays searchable. Deleting a chat also does not delete files saved separately in Library or Space.</Tip>

<Warning>
The setting has limits. Read them before you rely on it.

Turning it off covers new chats only. If you press thumbs up or thumbs down on a reply, OpenAI says the entire conversation linked to that feedback may be used to train its models, even if you opted out.

OpenAI may keep a copy of a temporary chat for up to 30 days for safety purposes. Authorised staff may access content for the stated reasons in the Why section.

Delete all chats cannot be undone, and a deleted chat cannot be restored. Delete one chat at a time unless you are sure.
</Warning>

<Safety>Never type a real password, one-time code, card number or ID number into a chat, even to try a setting. Use fictional examples like the one above. Do not paste other people's private details. For account help, use only the [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/).</Safety>

## If It Does Not Work

If you cannot see a setting, OpenAI says it may not be available for your account yet. Controls can differ by plan, region, platform and workspace.
