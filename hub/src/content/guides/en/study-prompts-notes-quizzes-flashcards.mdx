<TLDR>Paste your notes into ChatGPT or Gemini, then use these 12 prompts to summarize them, quiz yourself one question at a time, make flashcards and review what you missed. Answer each question yourself before you look at the answer. Check everything against your own notes.</TLDR>

<Problem>You have notes and a test coming up. You want to practice, not only reread. But you are not sure what to ask a chat tool, so you ask for "a summary" and get a long wall of text.</Problem>

<Why>
Carnegie Mellon's Eberly Center describes retrieval practice as asking students to recall information from memory. It says this can strengthen that memory.

The center counts quizzes, flashcards and practice problems as retrieval tasks. It also says that trying to recall something and getting it wrong can still help more than only rereading the right answer, as long as you get feedback.

It also says multiple choice tests may be less effective than short-answer tests, and it advises practice early and often. These prompts follow that idea. You answer first, then you get feedback.
</Why>

<Fix>Paste your notes, pick a prompt from the four groups below, and answer before you look. Then check against your notes.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Choose your notes.** Pick one topic and a page or two that you wrote yourself. Use sample notes or your own. Keep names, addresses and other private details out.</Step>
<Step>**Open ChatGPT or Gemini and add the notes.** In ChatGPT, select the + button, then Add photos & files, or paste the text into the message box. ChatGPT Free has a small daily limit on uploads, so pasting notes also works. {/* CHECK: OpenAI's upload page (read 2026-11-06) gives a number for the Free daily upload cap. ## Notes Prompts

Use these first. They turn a pile of notes into something short. We wrote all 12 prompts in this guide ourselves; they are not from OpenAI or Google. {/* CHECK: all 12 prompts are ours. No demo run is recorded, so this guide claims no result for any of them. Run each on a sample note before publishing. */}

<Prompt label="Summarize My Notes">Here are my notes on [topic]. Summarize them in plain words as 5 short bullet points. Use only what is in my notes. If something is unclear, say so. Do not guess.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Explain One Idea">Explain [idea from my notes] in simple words. Use one example. Then show me which line of my notes it comes from, so I can check it.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Find Gaps in My Notes">Read my notes on [topic]. List three questions that my notes do not answer. Do not answer them yet. I will look them up myself.</Prompt>

## Quiz Prompts

Each quiz prompt asks for one question at a time and tells the tool to wait. That keeps you answering instead of reading.

<Prompt label="Quiz Me One Question at a Time">Quiz me on my notes about [topic]. Ask one question at a time. Wait for my answer before you reply. Then tell me if I was right, explain why, and say what to review next. Start with question 1.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Short-Answer Quiz">Write 5 short-answer questions from my notes on [topic]. Ask them one at a time. Wait for my answer each time. After each answer, give feedback and point to the line in my notes.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Quiz With Hints">Quiz me on [topic], one question at a time. Wait for my answer. If I type "hint", give me a small clue, not the answer. If I get it wrong, let me try once more before you explain.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Quiz With Feedback at the End">Ask me 5 short-answer questions on [topic], one at a time. Wait for my answer each time. Do not say if I was right until the end. Then list what I got right, what I got wrong and what to review next.</Prompt>

## Flashcard Prompts

<Prompt label="Make Flashcards From My Notes">Make 10 flashcards from my notes on [topic]. Put a question on the front and a short answer on the back. Use only what is in my notes. Show them as a table with two columns.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Flash Me One Card at a Time">Act as my flashcards for [topic]. Show only the front of one card. Wait for my answer. Then show the back and ask if I got it right. Then show the next card.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Key Terms Cards">List the key terms in my notes on [topic]. For each term, make a card. Put the term on the front. Put a one-sentence plain definition on the back.</Prompt>

## Review Prompts

Keep a list of the questions you got wrong. These two prompts work from that list.

<Prompt label="Review What I Missed">Here are the questions I got wrong: [paste them]. Explain each one in simple words. Then ask me each question again, one at a time. Wait for my answer each time.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Plan My Next Practice Round">Here are the questions I missed: [paste them]. Make a short plan to practice them over the next [number] days. Give me a few questions for each day. Keep each day under [number] minutes.</Prompt>

<Tip>Gemini also has Guided Learning, which Google describes as using Gemini as a tutor. On a computer, click Add Files, then More tools, then Guided Learning. {/* CHECK: Google says it is rolling out features gradually, and the menu may differ by account. Show it only if it appears on the recording account. */} If your quiz comes back as multiple choice, ask for short answers instead.</Tip>

<Warning>A quiz can contain a wrong question or a wrong answer. OpenAI says study mode can make mistakes. Google says Gemini may miss connections or details in large uploads. Check every answer against your own notes before you rely on it.</Warning>

<Safety>
Use sample notes or notes you wrote yourself. Do not paste other people's private details, such as names, addresses, grades or account numbers.

What is kept, and whether it is used to improve a model, depends on your settings. {/* CHECK: OpenAI's data-use pages named on the upload page were not read for this guide (gap G5). Add the exact setting steps after reading them. */} In Gemini, saved chats and uploads count against a Gemini storage limit.

These prompts are a way to learn. They are not a way to hand in work that is not yours. For graded work, follow the AI-use policies of your school, instructor or organization.
</Safety>

## If It Does Not Work

- **The tool misread your file.** Upload a clearer version, paste the relevant text, or name the page or section to focus on.
- **You see no quiz shortcut in ChatGPT.** Ask in the chat instead: "Quiz me on these notes."
- **It gives you the answer straight away.** OpenAI says study mode can still give direct answers. Ask it to use hints, quizzes or step-by-step guidance.
- **You hit an upload limit.** Paste your notes into the message box instead.
- **A large Gemini upload gives a thin quiz.** Google says Gemini may miss connections or details. Upload a smaller file, or one topic at a time.
- **The feature is missing on your account.** Features can reach accounts at different times. Use the plain prompts above in a normal chat. {/* CHECK: no page read states Arabic support for these features (gap G2). Do not add a language claim in the EN or AR version without a demo. */}
