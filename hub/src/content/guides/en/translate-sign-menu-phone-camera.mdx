<TLDR>Open the Google Translate app, tap Camera, point it at the sign or menu, and tap Shutter to freeze the translation. You can also translate a saved photo, or just one phrase. Small, unclear or stylized text may not be translated correctly.</TLDR>

<Problem>You are standing in front of a sign or a menu in a language you cannot read. Typing it into a translator, letter by letter, is slow. You just want to know what it says.</Problem>

<Why>
Google says you can use your phone's camera to translate text in the world around you with the Translate app. Signs, menus and handwritten notes are some of its examples.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S5 relay) */}

You do not need to type the text. You point the camera at it.
</Why>

<Fix>Use the Camera inside the Google Translate app. Five short steps. Android and iPhone steps are kept apart.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>
**Install the app and choose your languages.**

**Android:** Download the Google Translate app from Google Play. Set the "From" language, or choose "Detect language". Set the "To" language.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 and S3 relay; "from Google Play" comes from our own step list) */}

**iPhone:** Install the Google Translate app from the App Store. Set the "From" and "To" languages. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S2 relay; no page read for the iPhone install step) */}
</Step>
<Step>
**Open the camera.**

**Android:** On the home screen, tap the Camera icon. To translate a photo you already have, tap All Images.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 relay) */}

**iPhone:** Tap Camera. To use a saved photo, tap Gallery instead. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S2 relay) */}
</Step>
<Step>
**Point at the sign or menu, then tap Shutter.**

**Android:** Point the camera at the text. The translation shows live. Tap the Shutter Button to freeze it.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 relay) */}

**iPhone:** Point the camera at the text and tap Shutter. The image freezes. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S2 relay) */}
</Step>
<Step>
**Translate just one phrase.**

**Android:** Tap Expand, then Translate options. Turn on "Show original text". Tap a word, or drag the anchors to cover a phrase. The translation appears in the card.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 relay) */}

**iPhone:** Tap Camera, then Shutter, then Translate options. Turn on "Show original text". Tap a word, or drag the anchors.
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S2 relay) */}

With text selected, you can Copy text, Listen, Send to Translate Home, or Search. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 and S2 relay) */}
</Step>
<Step>
**Optional: get ready for no signal.** Camera translation offline needs the languages downloaded first. Do this on Wi-Fi, before you travel.

**Android:** The download needs a Wi-Fi connection by default. A language you have not downloaded shows "Not available offline".
{/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S3 relay) */}

**iPhone:** On Wi-Fi, open Translate, tap a language, then tap Download. A downloaded language shows "Downloaded". You can also look under Settings, then Offline translation. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S4 relay) */}
</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Tip>For better results, check that the From and To languages are right, and use a strong internet connection. In low light, turn on Camera Flash. Clear, common fonts work best. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S1 and S2 relay) */}</Tip>

<Tip>We did not check which languages the camera accepts. Pick your languages in the app and see whether the camera works with them. On Android, Google says your device needs an auto-focus camera and a dual-core CPU with ARMv7. {/* CHECK: re-read on live page (S3 relay; camera language list not read) */}</Tip>

<Warning>Do not rely on camera translation for allergens, medicine labels, legal text or safety signs. Google says translation of small, unclear or stylized text may not be correct. Ask a person when a mistake could hurt someone.</Warning>

<Safety>Do not point the camera at other people's documents or IDs. We found no privacy statement about camera images on the pages we read, so we make no claim about where your camera images go.</Safety>
