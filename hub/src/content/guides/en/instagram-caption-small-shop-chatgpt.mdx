<TLDR>Give ChatGPT a few facts about your shop and ask for three short caption options. Edit your favorite by hand, add 3 to 5 hashtags and your own alt text, then paste it into Instagram. This guide uses a fictional shop, and the ChatGPT free plan has usage limits.</TLDR>

<Problem>You have a photo of something new in your shop. You open Instagram and the caption box is empty. You want words that sound like you, and you do not want to spend long finding them.</Problem>

<Why>
A chatbot can turn a few plain facts into several caption drafts. You start with something to edit instead of a blank box.

Meta for Business says AI-generated content is a starting point. It says to always review and personalize the output to match your brand voice before posting. So ChatGPT writes the draft, and you make the final call.
</Why>

<Fix>
Ask ChatGPT for three short drafts, edit one by hand, add hashtags and alt text, then share. Every example here uses a fictional shop called Corner Loaf Bakery. It is not a real business.

You need a ChatGPT account, an Instagram account and one photo. The ChatGPT free plan costs $0 and has no set time limit. It includes core features with usage limits, and the OpenAI page we read gives no exact numbers for them.
</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Open a new chat in ChatGPT.** Create a free account if you do not have one. Then start chatting on the web or in the mobile app. {/* CHECK: the sign-up and chat-box screens need the demo recording. */} Do not type customer names, phone numbers or orders into the chat.</Step>
<Step>**Send the shop facts.** Copy the first prompt below and replace the example facts with your own. ChatGPT replies with three caption options. Keep the facts short and plain.</Step>
<Step>**Pick one and edit it by hand.** Read all three options and choose the one closest to your voice. Add one real detail that only you know. Delete anything that is not true, such as a price, a review or a claim you cannot back up.</Step>
<Step>**Check the length and keep links out.** Meta for Business advises a strong opening line and a conversational tone. It also advises 125 characters or less, so the caption is fully visible without tapping More. {/* CHECK: no Instagram Help page read gives a caption length limit, so the demo should paste a long caption and record what happens. */} Meta also says you cannot add clickable links directly in captions. Any web address you type appears as plain text.</Step>
<Step>**Ask for hashtags and check them.** Copy the second prompt below. Meta for Business advises 3 to 5 hashtags that relate to your business, industry and the specific content of your post. Remove any that do not fit. Instagram says spaces and special characters like $ or % will not work in a hashtag. {/* CHECK: the Help Center wording on the hashtag count is unclear, so the demo should type 6 or more tags once and record what Instagram does. */} Instagram's hashtag page also warns that too many tags can stop a comment from posting, so stay at 3 to 5. You can add hashtags in the caption or in the comments.</Step>
<Step>**Start the post in Instagram.** On a computer, click Create on the left, then click Post. Pick your photo and click Next. Click Next again, then click in the box that says "Write a caption..." and paste your edited caption. {/* CHECK: the phone path ("Tap") is not confirmed and needs a demo before this guide covers phones. */} Do not share yet.</Step>
<Step>**Write the alt text yourself.** Before you share, click Accessibility. Write a short description of what the photo shows in the box. Use your own words and do not paste the caption. Instagram says a custom description is only read if someone is using a screen reader.</Step>
<Step>**Say that AI helped.** Be open about it. For example, add a short line such as "Caption drafted with AI and edited by us." Instagram's own AI label rule is narrower. The page we read says labeling images is not required, but you must manually label photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that has been digitally generated or altered. {/* CHECK: the label steps are shown for the app only, and the phone screens need a demo. */} We found no page that says an AI-written caption needs a label.</Step>
<Step>**Share the post, then fix it later if you need to.** Click Share. To change the caption afterward, click Options in the top right of your post, then click Edit. Change the text and click Done.</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Prompt label="Three Caption Options for a Fictional Shop">Write 3 short Instagram caption options for a small shop post. Shop: Corner Loaf Bakery, a fictional bakery. Product: fresh cinnamon rolls. Tone: warm and friendly. Language: English. Length: 125 characters or less each. Start each caption with a first line that says what is new. Do not invent prices, reviews or claims. Do not include any links.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Hashtags for the Chosen Caption">Suggest 3 to 5 hashtags for this caption: [paste your edited caption]. They should relate to a small bakery and to this post. Write each hashtag as one word with no spaces and no special characters.</Prompt>

<Warning>Do not post made-up reviews, testimonials, prices or sales claims. A draft may include them. Read every line and remove anything you cannot back up.</Warning>

<Safety>This guide uses a fictional shop on purpose. When you use your own shop, do not paste customer names, phone numbers or orders into ChatGPT. We did not read an OpenAI page about how chats are used, so we make no claim about it. Never share a password in a chat or in a caption. A person should read the caption and press Share, not the AI.</Safety>

<Tip>Put the offer in the first line. Meta for Business advises 125 characters or less, so the caption is fully visible without tapping More. If you add a line saying AI helped, it uses part of that length.</Tip>

## If It Does Not Work

- **ChatGPT writes captions that are too long.** Ask again and say "one short sentence each, 125 characters or less."
- **A hashtag will not post.** Remove any spaces or special characters, and cut the list back to 3 to 5.
- **A web address in the caption is not clickable.** This is expected. Meta says typed web addresses appear as plain text, so do not rely on a link in the caption.
- **ChatGPT stops replying.** The free plan has usage limits. Come back later, or write the caption yourself.
