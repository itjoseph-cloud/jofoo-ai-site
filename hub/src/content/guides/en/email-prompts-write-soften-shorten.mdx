<TLDR>Pick the prompt that matches your job: write, soften, shorten or reply. Copy it into ChatGPT on the free plan, fill in the brackets, and paste your message. Read the result, fix it, and only then send it.</TLDR>

## What You Need

- A ChatGPT account on the free plan. OpenAI says drafting emails is a Free use and that you create a free account to start.
- A message to work on, with private details removed.
- A moment to read the draft before it goes out.

Free has usage limits. OpenAI's pricing page also lists limited memory and context, and an input size of about 12 pages of text. Paid plans exist, and plan details change, so check the page before you rely on them. Checked on 5 November 2026.

We wrote all 10 prompts in this guide ourselves; they are not from OpenAI, Google or Microsoft. All names in this guide are made up.

<Problem>
You have to answer a message and the words will not come. Maybe a made-up sender, Dana Okoye, has written: "Where is my invoice? This is my third message." You want a calm reply.

Or your own draft sounds sharper than you meant. Or it runs on for paragraphs, and the reader may skim past the one thing you need.
</Problem>

<Why>
A blank page is slow. A draft can come out sharper than you intended. A long message can hide the one action you want the reader to take.

A chatbot can do the first pass on each of these. You then read it, change it and send it. You stay the author.

Shorter is not only a matter of taste. Digital.gov, a US government plain-language guide, says content is easier to understand when it uses shorter words, short sections, active voice and present tense. It adds that active voice makes it clear who should do what.
</Why>

<Fix>Use one prompt for one job. Copy it, fill in the brackets, add your message, then read and edit the result before you send.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Open ChatGPT.** Go to chatgpt.com or open the mobile app. OpenAI says you create a free account and start chatting on the web or in the app. Start a new chat for each message.</Step>
<Step>**Choose your privacy setting.** On the Free plan, "Improve the model for everyone" is on by default. {/* CHECK: relayed from the caller's 2026-11-05 check of help.openai.com; the quote was not re-captured. */} To turn it off, open Settings and choose Data controls. Or use Temporary chat, which OpenAI says is not used to improve models. It is kept for up to 30 days for safety. {/* CHECK: the app entry point for Temporary chat has not been recorded in a demo. */} Either way, do not paste passwords, ID numbers, or client or medical details.</Step>
<Step>**Pick a prompt.** Choose a job below: write, soften, shorten or reply. Press Copy, then paste it into the chat.</Step>
<Step>**Fill in the brackets and add your message.** Replace each [bracket] with a real detail. Be specific. Google gives this advice for its own Help me write tool: make your prompt as specific and detailed as you can. It is a good habit for any prompt. Where a prompt ends with [paste], add your message there.</Step>
<Step>**Read it, edit it, then send it.** Check names, dates and amounts. Make sure you still agree with every line. In Gmail, a short safety net helps: after you send, you see Message sent and Undo at the bottom left. </StepByStep>

## Write a New Email

Use these when you know the facts but not the words. Each prompt tells the chatbot not to add facts you did not give it.

<Prompt label="1. Write an Email From Your Facts">Write a short email from me to [recipient name]. The purpose is [what you want]. Include these facts: [date, amount or detail]. Keep the tone polite and direct. Use under [number] words. Do not add facts I did not give you.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="2. Ask for Something by a Date">Write an email asking [recipient name] for [what you need] by [date]. Explain why it matters in one sentence. Make the next step clear. Keep the tone friendly and professional. Do not pressure the reader.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="3. Follow Up Without Nagging">Write a follow-up email to [recipient name] about [topic]. I first wrote on [date] and have not had a reply. Ask one clear question. Stay polite and do not blame them.</Prompt>

## Soften a Message

Use these when your draft sounds harsher than you meant. A made-up example of the kind of line to paste in: "Third time asking. Send the invoice today."

<Prompt label="4. Soften a Blunt Draft">Rewrite the email below so it is kind and still clear. Keep every fact and my request. Remove anything that sounds angry or sharp. Do not add apologies I did not ask for. Email: [paste]</Prompt>

<Prompt label="5. Say No Politely">Write a polite email declining [request] from [recipient name]. Thank them first. Give this reason: [reason]. Offer this alternative: [alternative, or write none]. Keep it short and warm.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="6. Turn Criticism Into Useful Feedback">Turn my notes into kind, useful feedback for [recipient name]. Start with what works. Then say what to change and why. End by offering help. Do not be harsh and do not be vague. My notes: [paste]</Prompt>

## Shorten a Message

Use these on a long draft or a long thread. The free plan limits how much text you can paste, so paste only the part that matters.

<Prompt label="7. Shorten My Draft">Shorten the email below to under [number] words. Keep the main request, the dates and the amounts. Use short words and active voice. Show only the new version. Email: [paste]</Prompt>

<Prompt label="8. Boil a Thread Down">Read the email thread below. Give me three short bullet points on what matters, then one line on what I need to do next. Add nothing that is not in the thread. Thread: [paste]</Prompt>

## Reply to a Message

Use these when someone has written to you and you need an answer.

<Prompt label="9. Reply to an Email">Write a reply to the email below. My answer is [yes, no or later]. Include these points: [point 1] and [point 2]. Match the tone of the email I received. Use under [number] words. Email: [paste]</Prompt>

<Prompt label="10. Reply With Questions">Write a polite reply to the email below that asks for the details I am missing. List my questions as a numbered list. Keep it short. Do not make promises for me. Email: [paste]</Prompt>

<Safety>Keep private data out of any chat. Do not paste passwords, ID numbers, or client or medical details. Replace names and amounts with made-up ones if you are unsure. ChatGPT's pricing page lists use of your content to train models as opt-out on all plans. If you use Gmail's Help me write, know that Google says it takes details from your other emails and Drive files, so check what it wrote before you send. If your work or school has rules on AI help, follow them.</Safety>

<Warning>An AI draft can be wrong. Google says its Gemini features may suggest inaccurate or inappropriate information. Read every line before you send. Do not use these prompts to pose as someone else or to rush or pressure the reader.</Warning>

## If It Does Not Work

- **The reply is too long or the tone is off.** Tell the chatbot what to change in the same chat. For example: "Make it shorter" or "Make it warmer". You can also add a word limit to the prompt.
- **You hit a limit, or the thread is too long.** Free has usage limits and an input cap. Paste only the part of the thread that matters, or try again later.
- **You cannot find Help me write in Gmail.** It may not be available for your account or region. Use the ChatGPT prompts above instead.
