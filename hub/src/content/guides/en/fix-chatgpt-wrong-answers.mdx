<TLDR>Tell ChatGPT more about what you need, ask it to say when it is not sure, and ask it to show sources. Then pick three claims and check each at the original source before you use the answer.</TLDR>

<Problem>You ask ChatGPT a simple question. The answer sounds sure of itself, but it is wrong, vague, or not what you asked for. You start to wonder if you are doing something wrong.</Problem>

<Why>
ChatGPT writes answers from patterns it learned. OpenAI says it can give wrong or misleading answers, and it can sound confident even when it is wrong.

It can also miss the point when your question is short. It does not know who you are, why you are asking, or what kind of answer would help you.

Its knowledge also stops at a certain date. Unless it searches the web, it may not know about recent events.
</Why>

<Fix>Give it more to work with, and ask it to be honest about doubt. Then ask for sources and check the parts that matter.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Say what went wrong, and add context.** In the message box, tell ChatGPT what was wrong with the last answer. Then add who you are, what you need it for, and the format you want. For example: "That was too general. I am a beginner. Explain it in 5 short points."</Step>
<Step>**Ask it to say when it is not sure.** Add one line to your question: "If you are not sure, say so. Do not make up facts, dates, or links." This makes it easier to spot weak parts of the answer. It can still be wrong without saying so, so keep checking.</Step>
<Step>**Ask for sources.** For anything recent or factual, ask: "Search the web and show your sources." When ChatGPT searches, links to its sources appear in the answer. Open at least one link and see if it says the same thing. A link is not proof: OpenAI says ChatGPT may not be able to read a page because of technical issues, paywalls or site settings, so check the page yourself.</Step>
<Step>**Rewrite your question and ask again.** If the answer is still off, change your question. Add a detail, remove what is not needed, or ask a smaller question. You can also edit your last message or ask for a new answer. {/* SOURCED 2026-11-18: OpenAI release notes (help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453) describe editing a sent message and a retry menu under the reply. Exact controls vary by device and plan, so keep this sentence general. Name controls only after a recording shows them. */}</Step>
<Step>**Check three claims before you use it.** Pick three things in the answer that you can check: a name, a number, a date, a quote, or a link. OpenAI advises you to verify quotes, data, technical information and references. For each one, open the original source, such as a government or agency page, and see if it says the same. Mark each as "matches", "does not match", or "could not check". Fix or remove anything that does not match or that you could not check before you share it. If the answer affects your health, money, work, or plans, check it with a trusted source. Use ChatGPT as a first draft, not the final word.</Step>
</StepByStep>

**Our three-claim check:** launch date (April 24, 1990): matches. Shuttle (Discovery, STS-31): matches. Orbit height (about 340 miles): matches the page ChatGPT gave for it, does not match NASA's STS-31 page. As step 5 says, fix it: do not state it as plain fact, write what one page says and name it, for example "about 340 miles (547 km), according to NASA's page for students".

<Prompt label="Fix a Vague Answer">That answer was too general. I am a beginner and I need this for [your goal]. Please explain it again in 5 short points, in plain words. If you are not sure about something, say so. Do not make up facts, dates, or links.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Ask for Sources">Search the web for up-to-date information about [your question]. Give a short answer, then list the sources you used with links. Tell me if the sources disagree.</Prompt>

<Tip>Short questions get general answers. One extra sentence about who you are and why you are asking can help. OpenAI advises giving enough context.</Tip>

<Warning>A confident answer is not always a correct one. Always check quotes, numbers, dates, and links before you share or act on them.</Warning>
