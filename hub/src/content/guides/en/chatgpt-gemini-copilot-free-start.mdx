<TLDR>Open ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, paste the same made-up prompt into each, and keep the one that suits you. All three have a free plan at $0, and none states exact limits, so this guide ranks none of them. Type nothing personal, because all three may use or review chats in some cases.</TLDR>

<Problem>You keep hearing about ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot. All three say they are free. You do not know which one to open first, and you do not want to sign up for the wrong thing or pay by mistake.</Problem>

<Why>
There is no single answer, because the three free plans differ and they change. Google says Gemini limits may change without notice. What suits you also depends on your task and on how an answer reads to you.

Here is what each company says its free plan offers, as of 2026-11-05. It is a list, not a ranking.

- **ChatGPT.** OpenAI's [Free plan page](https://chatgpt.com/plans/free/) says anyone who creates an account can use it, with no set time limit. It says Free includes core features with usage limits, and it lists web search, uploads, study mode and image creation. OpenAI's [pricing page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing) marks uploads, images, voice, deep research and memory as limited. {/* CHECK: pricing card also lists text chats as unlimited with abuse guardrails; reader-quote, not placed. Name no model. */} OpenAI's [ads help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt) says ads may appear for users on the Free and Go plans in select regions. It also says ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers.
- **Gemini.** Google's [plans page](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/) lists the free plan at $0 a month with a Google Account. It includes image generation, Deep Research, Gemini Live, Canvas and Gems. Google's [limits page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805) says "Standard limits" apply, with a five-hour refresh until a weekly limit.
- **Copilot.** Microsoft's [support page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/what-s-the-difference-between-microsoft-copilot-free-and-copilot-in-microsoft-365) says Copilot is free of charge at copilot.cloud.microsoft, in apps or in Edge. It offers chat help for writing, brainstorming and summarizing. The page states no specific limits, and it says availability may vary by region.

None of the three publishes exact usage numbers. So the fair way to start is to try the same prompt in each and see which one feels right to you.
</Why>

<Fix>{/* CHECK: timeMinutes 15 is an estimate until a timed run exists. */} Open all three, read each data setting, paste one made-up prompt into each on the same day, and choose the one that suits you.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Open ChatGPT.** Go to chatgpt.com. OpenAI says the Free plan is for anyone who creates an account. Sign up or log in. If you see an upgrade offer, close it.</Step>
<Step>**Open Gemini.** Go to gemini.google.com. Google says some features work when you are signed out, and a Google Account gives you more features and saved activity. {/* CHECK: signed-out behaviour not yet recorded on screen. */} Sign in if you want your activity saved.</Step>
<Step>**Open Copilot.** Go to copilot.cloud.microsoft, or use the Copilot app or Microsoft Edge. Microsoft's pages differ on whether you must sign in, so check on the day. {/* CHECK: C4 vs C5 conflict (support page says sign-in is not required; terms say a Microsoft Account is needed). Confirm on a signed-out screen. */} Sign in if it asks you to.</Step>
<Step>**Read ChatGPT's data setting before you type.** Open Settings and select Data controls. OpenAI says that on personal workspaces with ChatGPT Free, Plus or Pro, data sharing is on by default. You can turn off "Improve the model for everyone" and keep your chat history. OpenAI also says temporary chats are not used to improve its models and may be kept for up to 30 days for safety.</Step>
<Step>**Read Gemini's data setting.** Look for the Keep Activity setting. {/* CHECK: menu path for Keep Activity not recorded; add it after the demo. */} Google says that with it off, activity is not saved or used for training unless you send feedback.</Step>
<Step>**Read Copilot's data setting.** Open the privacy settings. Microsoft describes "Training on conversation activity" and "Training on voice conversations" for the older app, and says opting out does not stop every other use. {/* CHECK: Microsoft changed the app on 2026-08-18; the updated-app privacy path is unknown. Confirm on screen. */} Microsoft also says it stores conversation activity for 18 months by default.</Step>
<Step>**Paste the same prompt into all three.** Use the sample prompt below, one tool after another, on the same day. Do not change a word. Do not add real names or details.</Step>
<Step>**Compare the answers and choose.** Ask yourself three things. Did it follow what I asked? Was it easy to read? Would I open it again? Keep the one that suits you. If two feel the same, use either one. You can switch later.</Step>
<Step>**Check the limits, and do not upgrade.** In Gemini, Google points to Usage Limits in Settings. {/* CHECK: Settings > Usage Limits is a reader paraphrase of the Google page; confirm on screen. */} OpenAI and Microsoft publish no numbers for their free plans. If you see a limit message, read it and stop. Do not click an upgrade button, and do not add a payment method.</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Prompt label="Sample Prompt to Paste in All Three">Write a friendly three-line message inviting the members of a made-up book club to meet on Thursday at 6 pm. Then list two things each person should bring.</Prompt>

<Safety>Type nothing personal into any of the three. Microsoft says humans may review your conversations in Copilot, and that an opt-out of human review is not available. Google says a subset of reviewed chats is kept for up to three years. OpenAI says data sharing is on by default on ChatGPT Free. Use made-up details only. Leave out real names, passwords, ID numbers, health details and money details.</Safety>

<Safety>Use a sign-in you are comfortable with, and add no payment method. If a child will use any of these, read the company's age rules first. Microsoft says you must be at least 13 to use Copilot, and the age can be higher in some countries. Google says Gemini needs you to be 13 or older for a personal or school account, and 18 or older for a work account. {/* CHECK: OpenAI age rule not verified; do not state one. Minors: founder approval needed. */} Check OpenAI's own rules for ChatGPT.</Safety>

<Warning>Microsoft says "Copilot can make mistakes, and it may not work as intended." Google also says Gemini can be wrong. Check any answer that matters before you use it.</Warning>

<Tip>Not sure after one prompt? Try a second made-up prompt of a different kind, such as a summary of a short made-up note. Use the same one in all three.</Tip>

## If a Free Plan Hits Its Limit

Read the message and wait. Google says Gemini limits refresh every five hours until a weekly limit. OpenAI and Microsoft publish no numbers, so a limit can come without warning. You can try one of the other two, or come back later. Do not click upgrade.
