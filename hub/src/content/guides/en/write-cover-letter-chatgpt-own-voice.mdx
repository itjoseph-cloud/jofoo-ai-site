<TLDR>Turn on a privacy option first, then give ChatGPT your true details and a short sample of your own writing, and ask for a draft. Edit it by hand and check every claim about you before you send it. ChatGPT can help you draft the letter, but it cannot promise an interview or a job.</TLDR>

<Problem>You have a job posting open and a blank page. You want a letter that sounds like you, not like a template. You also do not want to hand your name, address and work history to a chatbot without thinking first.</Problem>

<Why>
OpenAI says [ChatGPT can help you write a cover letter](https://chatgpt.com/learn/cover-letter/) that highlights your work experience and skills. It can also customise letters for different applications.

But ChatGPT only knows what you tell it. It can also add skills or results you do not have. The University of Michigan Career Center says that [while AI helps, personalization and creativity remain important](https://careercenter.umich.edu/content/cover-letter-resources).

Your details need care too. OpenAI says [data sharing is on by default](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt) for ChatGPT Free. You can turn it off. That is why privacy comes first in this guide.
</Why>

<Fix>Pick a privacy option first. Then give ChatGPT true details and a sample of your own writing. Ask for a draft, refine it, and edit it yourself. You stay in charge of what the letter says.</Fix>

<StepByStep>
<Step>**Pick a privacy option before you paste anything. Option A: turn off training.** Choose A or B, then go on. On the web, open your account menu, select Settings, select Data controls, turn off Improve the model for everyone, and select Done. {/* CHECK: toggle label and position are from OpenAI help text only; confirm on screen in the demo. */} On mobile, open the sidebar, select your profile icon to open Settings, select Data controls, and turn off the same setting. OpenAI says this does not delete or hide your saved chats. If you use ChatGPT without signing in, the choice is saved in that browser.</Step>
<Step>**Privacy option B: use a Temporary chat instead.** Open a new chat and select Temporary. Choose Unpersonalized before you send your first message, because you cannot change it later. OpenAI says Unpersonalized does not use memory or custom instructions. It also says Temporary chats are not used to improve its models and may be kept for up to 30 days for safety. **An unsaved Temporary chat cannot be reopened after you leave it, so copy your final letter out before you go.**</Step>
<Step>**Keep your name and contact details out of the chat.** Write [YOUR NAME] and [YOUR CONTACT DETAILS] wherever they belong. {/* CHECK: this is editorial advice from the privacy sources, not a rule OpenAI states. */} Leave out other people's details too, such as a manager's name. You will add the real details yourself at the end.</Step>
<Step>**Gather what the letter needs.** Write down the job title, the company, and the text of the job posting. Pick two or three of your own experiences that match the posting. List your key skills. The careers office says to use specific examples and results, and not to repeat your resume. Use only what is true.</Step>
<Step>**Ask for a first draft.** Copy the prompt called First Draft below. Fill in the brackets and send it. OpenAI says to include your experience, your key skills and what the job description is looking for. You can also upload your resume and the job description. {/* CHECK: the upload control and Free-plan upload behaviour have not been seen on screen. */} A resume usually holds your contact details, so upload it only after step 1 or 2. OpenAI says uploads have separate usage limits on the Free plan.</Step>
<Step>**Add your own voice. This step is our own method.** Write a few sentences yourself, such as part of an old email or a school essay. Keep private details out of it. Paste it with the prompt called Match My Writing below. {/* CHECK: no OpenAI page read describes voice matching; keep the "our method" label. */} We did not find this step on any OpenAI page. Results vary, so compare the new draft with your sample and keep only what sounds like you.</Step>
<Step>**Refine the draft.** Ask for changes in a follow-up message. Use the prompt called Refine the Draft below, or ask for your own change. The careers office says a cover letter should not exceed one page.</Step>
<Step>**Check the draft against a careers-office list.** It should have 3 to 4 paragraphs. The first says why you are writing and why you want this role or organization. The middle paragraphs give specific examples and results. The last one thanks the employer for their time. Name the person you are writing to, because the careers office says to avoid "To Whom It May Concern". Cut any cliché. Then read the letter aloud and edit it by hand until it sounds like you.</Step>
<Step>**Check every claim about you.** Read each skill, job, date and result in the letter. If it is not true, or you could not explain it in an interview, change it or delete it. ChatGPT can add things you did not say.</Step>
<Step>**Copy the final letter into your own document.** Add your real name, contact details, the date and the person's name yourself. Check spelling and grammar once more. The careers office says a letter should be error-free. If you used a Temporary chat, copy the text before you leave it.</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Prompt label="First Draft (The Example Details Are Made Up)">Help me draft a cover letter. Job title: [JOB TITLE, for example Library Assistant]. Company: [COMPANY]. Key points from the job posting: [PASTE THE JOB DESCRIPTION]. My experience that matches: [TWO OR THREE TRUE EXPERIENCES, for example ran a school book club]. My key skills: [YOUR TRUE SKILLS]. Use only the facts I gave you. Do not add skills or results I did not mention. Leave my name and contact details as [YOUR NAME] and [YOUR CONTACT DETAILS]. Keep it to one page.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Match My Writing (Our Method)">Here is a short text I wrote myself: [PASTE YOUR OWN WRITING]. Rewrite the cover letter so its tone and sentence length feel like this text. Keep every fact the same. Do not add anything new about me.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Refine the Draft">Can you make the tone more friendly? Let's cut it down to 150 words.</Prompt>

<Prompt label="Reusable Template">Turn this cover letter into a template I can use later. Put brackets around the information I need to remember to update.</Prompt>

<Warning>Copy your final letter out before you leave a Temporary chat. An unsaved one cannot be reopened. If you save it, it becomes a regular chat and follows your account's training setting.</Warning>

<Safety>A cover letter holds your name, employer, contact details and work history. Keep them out of the chat and add them yourself at the end. Do not press thumbs up or thumbs down on a chat that has personal text. OpenAI says the whole conversation tied to that feedback may be used to train its models, even if you turned training off. If you cannot change the training setting, a parent, guardian or workspace control may be limiting it.</Safety>

<Safety>Only you can say what is true about you. ChatGPT can add skills or results you do not have, and you are the one who signs the letter. Some employers or schools may have rules on AI-written applications. {/* CHECK: no primary source read on employer AI policies; this is a prompt to check, not a stated rule. */} Check the posting or ask before you send. This guide cannot promise an interview or a job.</Safety>

<Tip>Applying to more than one role? Send the Reusable Template prompt after your final draft, then fill in the brackets for each new job. If a long chat fills up, start a new chat and paste in the passage you want to keep working on. Choose your privacy option again in the new chat. Free plan limits can change, and ads may appear in some countries, so check what your own screen shows.</Tip>
